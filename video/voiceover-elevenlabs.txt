DriverScore demo video: voiceover script (draft 3)
Read in one take, one paragraph per scene, in this order. Recorded with Reed's voice, ElevenLabs v4.
To re-record: generate the whole text, download the take, then run voice.py (see README.md).

[01-open]
Millions of older drivers, and the families who love them, face the same question. Can I still drive?

[02-problem]
In the United States, fifty-two million licensed drivers are sixty-five or older. Yet only a few hundred certified driver rehabilitation specialists serve the whole country. And the evidence is scattered: in the doctor's notes, at the pharmacy, in last year's eye exam. So decisions come late, on worry instead of evidence.

[03-solution]
DriverScore brings it together in one record: her health record, her own answers, and simulator drives chosen for her. A clinician reviews it all and signs one plan.

[04-home]
Before the visit, Margaret connects her health record through HealthKey, and chooses exactly what to share. Then she answers four short questions about her driving.

[05-checkin]
At the hospital, she checks in on her phone. On the clinician's iPad, what matters most comes first: a new sleep medicine, a cataract, and a close call.

[06-assign]
From her record, the clinician assigns the drives that matter. Brake reaction, for the new sleep medicine. A night drive in low light, for the cataract. Left turns, where most close calls happen. Margaret sees which drives were chosen, and why.

[07-drive]
Then she drives only what was assigned: six short simulator drives. As each one finishes, its score appears on the clinician's iPad.

[08-record]
Every result is saved to her health record, with what was measured and the rule behind each score.

[09-review]
Each score shows what was measured, the rule, and the result. DriverScore drafts a recommendation and shows its reasoning. The clinician makes the decision, edits the note, and signs.

[10-clear]
Margaret's plan arrives on her phone. She's clear to drive. Each drive is explained in plain words, with her next steps and her next re-test.

[11-outcomes]
Every driver gets one of four outcomes, each with a plan. Clear. Drive with conditions, like daylight only. Pause and reassess, when a problem can be treated. And time to stop. Many causes can be treated, so there's often a path back to driving.

[12-stop]
When it's time to stop, the clinician explains why, in person. Then her plan leads with how she'll get around: a ride with her daughter, a taxi, a senior shuttle, or a self-driving car.

[13-trust]
And it's built with safeguards. The clinician always decides. Margaret controls what she shares, and her records are never sold. DriverScore never reports to the DMV on its own.

[14-close]
DriverScore. One clinician-signed plan for every older driver.
